# Listen: wording for approval

Nothing here is built yet. Every line below is new wording of ours; Scripture is never re-worded. Mark any change and I will build exactly what you approve.

## 1. Spoken book names (81)

Each book has two spoken forms:

- **Chapter name**: said before every chapter.
- **Opening name**: said once, only when a book begins.

Brackets are never read. Numbered books are spoken as words ("First", "Second"). The rule for the opening name is **"The Book of {chapter name}"**, except the eight marked ★.

### Why the ★ entries differ

- **Psalms.** Each chapter is one psalm, so I use the WEB's own chapter label, "Psalm": "Psalm 23, verses 1 through 6", not "Psalms, chapter 23".
- **Song of Solomon, Wisdom of Solomon, Prayer of Manasses.** "The Book of Song of Solomon" sounds wrong, so each keeps "The" with its own title.
- **Psalm 151.** "The Book of Psalm 151" sounds wrong, so it stays "Psalm 151".
- **Greek Esther and Daniel.** These follow the shelf line already on screen ("the Greek forms of those books"). Another option is "Greek Esther" / "The Greek Book of Esther".
- **Other option for numbered books.** The older form, "The First Book of Samuel" and "The First Book of Maccabees", for all numbered books. Tell me if you prefer it.

## 2. Chapter announcements

These are spoken, not shown on screen. The first and last verse numbers are taken from the chapter itself.

| Case | Spoken |
|---|---|
| Any chapter | "Genesis, chapter 1, verses 1 through 31." |
| Psalms | "Psalm 23, verses 1 through 6." |
| One-verse chapter (none in the WEB today; the rule is built anyway) | "{Chapter name}, chapter {n}, verse 1." |
| One-chapter book | "Obadiah, verses 1 through 21." · "Psalm 151, verses 1 through 7." · "Third John, verses 1 through 14." |
| A book begins | The opening name first, then the chapter line: "The Book of Genesis. Genesis, chapter 1, verses 1 through 31." |
| A one-chapter book begins | "The Book of Obadiah. Obadiah, verses 1 through 21." |

Notes:

- **Sirach 26.** Verses 19 to 27 are omitted in the WEB. The announcement still uses the numbers the chapter has: "Sirach, chapter 26, verses 1 through 29". The omitted verses are silent.
- **Psalm titles.** For example, "A Psalm by David, when he fled from Absalom his son." They are read after the announcement and before verse 1, as you decided.
- **For your call: Psalm 119.** The WEB marks its Hebrew letter headings ("ALEPH", "BETH" and so on) the same way as Psalm titles, so the player would read them. Should it read them, or skip them?
- **What is never read:** verse numbers, translators' notes, cross-references, section headings, "BOOK 1" to "BOOK 5", speaker labels in Song of Solomon, and the Prologue of Sirach.
- **Brackets.** The square brackets in Esther (Greek) are not read. The words inside them are read.
- **Where reading stops.** Reading carries on from chapter to chapter and book to book. It stops after Revelation and after Daniel (Greek), the last book on its shelf. Malachi runs on into Matthew. Then it says: "This is the end of the {shelf}." For example, "This is the end of the New Testament."

## 4. About page additions

**How it works:** a new paragraph after the Bible study paragraph.

> Listen reads any chapter aloud with a voice stored on your device, verse after verse, chapter after chapter and book after book, until you stop it. It reads the words of Scripture and the Psalm titles. It does not read verse numbers, translators' notes, cross-references or other headings.

**Privacy:** a new paragraph.

> Listen uses only voices stored on your device, so your device speaks the words itself and nothing is sent anywhere. It never uses online voices, such as Google's or Microsoft's online voices. If your device has no stored voice, the app says so and does not show the player.

**Privacy, the Bible study sentence** (the kept-on-device list). The changed part is in bold:

> Bible study remembers the last chapter you read, **where listening stopped,** and a few **choices**, such as hiding the translators' notes **and the speed, voice, chapter announcements and Keep screen on setting for Listen**, on this device only, so you can carry on where you left off. Books you open are kept on this device so you can read them offline. Neither is ever sent anywhere.

**Sources, the "words that are ours" sentence.** It ends with this added clause:

> …and the labels around the cross-references**, and the labels, messages, spoken book names and chapter announcements in Listen**.

## 5. Limits I will state plainly (already in the wording above)

- iPhones and most Android phones stop browser speech when the screen locks or you switch apps. That is why there are "Continue from" and Keep screen on.
- On Android the speech engine belongs to the phone. The app lists only the voices the phone reports as stored on the device. It cannot look inside the engine.
